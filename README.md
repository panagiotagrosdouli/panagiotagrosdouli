## Research profile

My research interests lie at the intersection of **robotics, probabilistic perception, motion prediction, sensor fusion, and dependable artificial intelligence**. I am particularly interested in autonomous systems that operate with incomplete observations, changing sensor quality, uncertain environment models, and rare but safety-critical events.

My long-term goal is to contribute to research on **dependable autonomy**: systems that do not merely achieve strong average performance, but can also quantify uncertainty, detect degradation, explain failure modes, and recover safely when their assumptions no longer hold.

## Research questions

I am currently exploring questions such as:

- How can autonomous robots plan and re-plan when perception and future motion are uncertain?
- How can visual–inertial and multi-modal estimation systems detect inconsistency before failure becomes critical?
- How should trajectory-prediction models evaluate rare, high-impact behavior rather than only average cases?
- How can sensing, communication, and illumination be jointly adapted in dynamic environments?
- Which experimental practices make AI and robotics results genuinely reproducible and scientifically defensible?

## Methodological principles

- **Reproducibility:** deterministic configurations, documented assumptions, tests, metrics, and generated artefacts.
- **Uncertainty awareness:** predictions and state estimates should communicate confidence, not only point estimates.
- **Failure-oriented evaluation:** average performance should be complemented by stress tests, tail cases, degradation scenarios, and limitations.
- **Transparent evidence:** conceptual, synthetic, dataset-based, simulation, ROS 2, and hardware results should be clearly distinguished.
- **Responsible claims:** research prototypes should not be presented as certified safety mechanisms or formal guarantees.
